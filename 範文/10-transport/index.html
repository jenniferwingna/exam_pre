　　有意見認為，政府應在網約車投入運作後，盡快按市場需求提高數目上限。判斷此說，須先釐清規管的政策目的。政府一貫以公共交通為本，點對點服務屬輔助；規管網約車，是要令市民在不同情境下都叫得到安全、可問責的車，而不是單純增加路面車輛。由此可導出三項評估標準：一是供應最緊絀的時段和地區能否改善；二是道路承載力和公共交通生態不受損害；三是點對點行業能否健康持續。「市場需求」只回答了第一項的一部分。本文不同意在制度起步階段即按市場需求提高上限，而主張建立以服務指標觸發、分區分時段的調整機制：待數據證明一萬輛的運力未達指定服務水平，才按缺口增發。

　　就現況而言，規管框架已經到位，但三項關鍵資訊仍是空白。其一，上限是否不足，目前無從判斷。許可證要到第四季才開始發出，在「人車綁定」、第三者保險和車齡限制下，有多少車主願意申請、每輛車實際提供多少服務，要運作後才知道；現在談放寬，等於未量度供應便先判定不足。其二，癥結是錯配多於總量。叫車難集中在繁忙時段、惡劣天氣、深夜和偏遠地區，而司機都會流向收入最高的時段和地段；單純放寬總數，新增車輛很可能仍擠在市區，既加劇擠塞，又補不到缺口。其三，數據不對稱。需求數據掌握在平台手上，運輸署的系統仍在構建；平台有誘因放大需求，以其口中的市場需求為準，等於把發牌決定交給受規管者。

　　針對上述癥結，本文建議分三步推行。第一，先訂服務指標，再談數目。運輸署應在2027年上半年公布點對點服務指標，包括分區分時段的候車時間、叫車失敗率及偏遠地區接單率，並把定期提交數據寫入平台牌照條件；首十二個月為基線期，期內不調整上限。第二，以指標觸發、按缺口增發。基線期後，若個別地區或時段持續未達標，而許可證已接近用盡，才增發，並附服務條件，例如須在新界、離島或深夜提供指定比例的服務；同時以繁忙路段車速作「擠塞閘門」，車速跌破既定水平即暫停增發。第三，的士與網約車同步提升。的士司機已可經合併筆試簡便申領網約車許可證；下一步應鼓勵的士全面接入召車平台，並以同一套指標公開評核兩者。

　　上述措施在執行上會遇到三項挑戰。第一，數據質素與平台合規。平台可能選擇性提交數據，甚至透過派單令指標看似惡化。處理方法是訂明數據欄位和提交頻率，由運輸署抽查審計，並以政府車速數據和乘客問卷交叉核對；違規紀錄與續牌掛鈎。第二，調整機制淪為政治角力。平台會嫌慢，的士業界會嫌快。處理方法是事先公布指標、門檻和檢討周期，每半年公開評估結果，令決定可預期、可覆核；另設由的士、平台和乘客代表組成的諮詢小組，但決定權歸運輸署。第三，分區分時段監察加重運輸署的行政負擔。處理方法是先粗後細：首階段只分數個大區及繁忙、非繁忙兩類時段，並以系統自動比對數據。

　　或有意見認為，設上限就是保護既得利益：限額只會延續叫車難，並令許可證變成稀缺資產，重蹈的士牌照的覆轍。此說有其道理，稀缺牌照確是的士業困局的根源之一。惟「人車綁定」已杜絕許可證轉手和出租，稀缺不會變成炒賣價值；更重要的是，每多一輛載客私家車，擠塞成本由全體道路使用者承擔，包括人數多得多的巴士乘客，這項成本不反映在乘客與平台的交易中，市場需求因而必然高估社會需要。本文並非主張永久封頂，而是要求每次增發都有數據依據。

　　要爭取的士業界接受，關鍵在於回應他們真正擔心的兩件事：收入會否被新供應攤薄，以及規管是否厚此薄彼。對前者，本方案比「按市場需求」對業界有利得多：增發須通過公開的服務指標和擠塞閘門，業界可預先看見規則，而不是面對一個隨平台游說而變動的數字。對後者，政府應作出兩項承諾：其一，兩者以同一套指標評核，增發檢討亦同時檢視的士規管中不合時宜的限制，例如研究的士經平台預約時的收費彈性；其二，的士業界在諮詢小組中佔一席，並可取得匯總後的供求數據。新增需求最終流向善用平台的司機，而的士司機正是最快可取得網約車資格的一群。

　　歸根究柢，網約車上限的意義不在數字本身，而在調整數字的方法。以數據為閘門、以錯配為目標，既能逐步提升點對點服務，也能守住公共交通為本的原則。

　　就現況而言，香港的自動車正由測試走向個別商業項目，首個商業項目選在機場「航天走廊」，正是路線固定、行人稀少的環境。這個起點本身已說明，擴展至一般道路至少受三項限制。第一，責任未明。無人駕駛時發生事故，責任在製造商、營運商還是遙距操作員，現行安排並不完整；責任不清，保險業便缺乏定價基礎，商業營運隨之卡住。第二，人手效益未現。測試和初期營運多配備安全員，人手需求不減反增。第三，路況複雜而風險不對稱。小巴和巴士路線要處理頻密上落客、違例泊車、斜路急彎和密集人流，與機場範圍截然不同；而一宗嚴重事故引起的公眾抗拒，足以令整條技術路線停擺數年，收益卻只能逐步累積。

　　針對上述限制，本文建議三項措施。第一，按場景分級，以安全數據作閘門。運輸署應公布分級路線圖：第一級為機場、口岸人工島等封閉或專用範圍；第二級為固定路線的低速接駁，例如接駁高鐵西九龍站與機場快線九龍站的路線；第三級才是一般道路的公共交通。每升一級，須達到公開的安全指標，例如人工接管頻率、事故率和累積無嚴重事故里程。第二，先立法釐清責任，再談無人化。運輸及物流局宜在兩年內提出修例，訂明無人駕駛模式下營運商為首要責任人，須先行賠償再向製造商追討，並訂明遙距操作員的資格和每人監察車數的上限；同時與保險業界設計試驗期的分層保險，以營運商提交的事故及接管數據累積理賠基礎。第三，優先補位而非取代。第二級路線應優先選在司機最難招聘的時段和地區，例如深夜接駁和新發展區內的循環線，並設立轉型通道，資助職業司機受訓成為安全員、遙距操作員或車隊調度員。

　　上述措施在執行上會遇到三項挑戰。第一，業界嫌慢。分級閘門令擴展速度取決於數據，企業可能轉往政策較寬鬆的城市。處理方法是公布每級的準入條件和預計審批時間，令「慢」變得可預期；並容許營運商在同一級別內擴大車隊，先讓商業模式跑通。第二，立法需時，保險業仍嫌數據不足。處理方法是以牌照條件先行過渡，要求營運商承擔先行賠償責任，並由政府與保險業共享匿名化的測試及事故數據。第三，公眾信任。處理方法是定期公開各級營運數據，設立獨立事故調查機制，並預先訂明事故後的處理程序：只暫停涉事路線、調查、公布結果，避免一宗事故拖垮整個計劃。

　　歸根究柢，「粵車南下」的成敗不在名額多寡，而在每一個名額能否轉化為順暢的行程和實在的消費。按日子分層、以轉乘分流、以數據加碼，擴展才能既穩步又可持續。

CASE BACKGROUND

The City of Jade is developing the Westbank New Development Area (NDA), a former agricultural plain about 25 km from the city centre. The first 8,000 residents will move into public housing estates in 2028. The population is projected to reach 180,000 by 2036, with 50,000 jobs in a planned logistics and technology cluster, although the timing of the cluster depends on private investment.

A heavy-rail extension to Westbank was studied and found to cost about four times as much as the alternative; it could not open before 2038. The Government has therefore decided to build a 14-km medium-capacity "smart green transit" line, using rubber-tyred, driverless vehicles running on a mix of elevated track and reserved lanes on the NDA's main road. Phase 1 is scheduled to open in 2031. Its design capacity is about one-sixth of that of a heavy-rail line.

The line will terminate at Northgate Station on the existing rail network. Northgate's platforms already operate at 95 per cent of capacity in the morning peak, and the rail operator has said it cannot add trains on that line before 2033.

The tender for Phase 1 closed in June. Only two bids were received. The tender period had been extended once after potential bidders asked for clarification of the safety approval requirements, which the City has never had to set for this type of system. The two bids use different technologies, neither of which is in service elsewhere in the City.

A residents' group has collected 12,000 signatures calling for "a real railway, not a second-class tram". Franchised bus operators have asked whether their existing routes to Westbank will be cut when the line opens.

QUESTION

You work in the Ministry of Transport of the Government of the City of Jade. You have been tasked to draft an internal discussion paper on public transport provision for the Westbank New Development Area, in which you should:—

(a) set out two major problems facing the introduction of the medium-capacity smart green transit system in Westbank and explain the implication of the identified issues; and

(b) recommend feasible measures to address the major problems identified.

Please draft the paper in English.

DISCUSSION PAPER PUBLIC TRANSPORT FOR THE WESTBANK NEW DEVELOPMENT AREA: INTRODUCTION OF THE SMART GREEN TRANSIT SYSTEM

PURPOSE

  1. This paper (a) sets out two major problems facing the introduction of the medium-capacity smart green transit system in the Westbank New Development Area (NDA) and explains their implications; and (b) recommends measures to address them.

BACKGROUND

  1. The policy objective of the system is not simply to build a line. It is to give Westbank residents a reliable public transport journey to jobs and services from the outset, at a cost the City can sustain, so that the NDA grows as a public-transport-oriented community rather than a car-dependent one. The test of the project is therefore the quality of the whole journey, not the line in isolation.

  2. Measured against that test, the choice of a medium-capacity system over heavy rail is defensible. The risks lie elsewhere: at the point where the line meets the existing network, and in the City's ability to procure and approve a type of system it has never operated.

PROBLEM 1 — THE INTERCHANGE BOTTLENECK AT NORTHGATE

  1. A journey from Westbank to the city centre is only as fast as its most congested segment. The new line will deliver passengers to Northgate, whose platforms are already close to capacity in the morning peak, with no additional trains possible before 2033. For trips into town, the line adds demand to a node that cannot absorb it rather than adding capacity. Residents will also face a transfer and, unless fares are integrated, a second fare.

  2. Implications. The immediate implication is crowding at Northgate, a safety risk as well as a reliability problem. It will also fall on existing passengers from other districts, an equity cost borne by people who gain nothing from the project. The second-order implication is behavioural. Travel habits are formed in the first months after a household moves; if early residents find the journey slow and uncertain, they will buy cars, and such choices are rarely reversed. The NDA would then lock in car dependence on roads not designed for it. Finally, the residents' "second-class tram" campaign would be validated by experience rather than answered by argument. Pressure to revisit heavy rail, already rejected on cost, would grow, with the risk that the City ends up paying for both systems.

PROBLEM 2 — DELIVERING A FIRST-OF-ITS-KIND SYSTEM

  1. The City has no safety approval regime for driverless, rubber-tyred transit and is writing one while the tender is live. Only two bids were received, using different technologies, neither proven locally. The difficulty is institutional rather than technical: the regulator, the procurer and the market are all learning at the same time.

  2. Implications. The first is delay. Approval requirements settled mid-procurement tend to extend programmes, and any slippage beyond 2031 widens the gap between population intake and service that already exists from 2028. The second is cost: with two bidders, competitive tension is weak, and later design changes will be priced without it. The third, and least visible, is lock-in. The system chosen will define interfaces, spare parts and software for decades, possibly for future lines as well; a proprietary choice now constrains every extension and upgrade later. Lastly, an early safety incident would damage confidence not only in this line but in the medium-capacity approach generally.

RECOMMENDATIONS

  1. Treat Northgate as part of the project (Problem 1). The interchange should be scoped and funded within the project budget: additional platform access, concourse capacity and crowd management, agreed with the rail operator within six months. A peak-hour express bus to the city centre bypassing Northgate would divert part of the demand.

  2. Integrate fares and build habits early (Problem 1). Through-ticketing with an interchange discount should apply from opening day. The reserved lanes on the NDA main road should be completed first and used by buses from the first intake in 2028, so that residents start with public transport. Bus operators should receive a published route restructuring plan that redeploys buses to feeder and express roles rather than simply withdrawing them.

  3. Safeguard an upgrade path (Problems 1 and 2). The alignment and station sites should be protected for extension or conversion to higher capacity, with published triggers, for example sustained peak loading above a stated level. This answers the residents' concern with a transparent mechanism rather than a promise.

  4. Settle the rules before awarding the contract (Problem 2). The Ministry should designate the safety regulator, adopt a recognised international standard for this class of system and publish the approval pathway, supported by an independent safety assessor.

  5. Design the contract against lock-in (Problem 2). Specifications should be performance-based (capacity, availability, energy use), with open interfaces and guaranteed access to software and spare parts for the life of the asset. Separating civil works from systems and operation would let the long-lead civil works proceed while the systems market is re-engaged to attract more bidders. This is not a costless option: re-tendering the systems package may add several months. The alternative, a weakly contested contract for an untested system, carries a higher long-term cost.

IMPLEMENTATION AND MONITORING

  1. It is proposed that the approval framework and the Northgate works scope be finalised within six months; that bus services on the reserved lanes start with the first intake in 2028; and that the systems contract be awarded thereafter, maintaining the 2031 target for Phase 1. Progress should be reported quarterly to the Ministry's steering group against five indicators: peak platform occupancy at Northgate; door-to-door journey time to the city centre; the public transport share of trips by new residents; system availability after opening; and cost and schedule variance.

ADVICE SOUGHT

  1. Members are invited to comment on the problems identified in paragraphs 4 to 7 and the recommendations in paragraphs 8 to 12.
